Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Spokane Restoration Co know it well. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

How We Handle Structural Drying

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Equipment Deployment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

Final readings are documented, the equipment comes out and your property is certified at dry standard as soon as all affected materials test at acceptable levels.

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
