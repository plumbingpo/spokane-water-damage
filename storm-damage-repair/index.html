Wind-driven rain pushes water in through damaged roofing, broken windows and loose siding. Once the roof leaks, water saturates wall cavities, ceilings and insulation. During a flash flood, drains cannot keep up and water ends up in the lowest floors. A single restoration plan from our team covers each of these related issues together.

How We Handle Storm Damage Repair

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle storm damage repair from start to finish:

1

Emergency Board-Up & Tarping

Our crew covers damaged roofing, boards up shattered windows and seals openings, which keeps additional water out until permanent repairs can be planned.

2

Extract Water and Assess Damage

Our team pumps and vacuums out the water that came in with the storm. We then assess all affected areas with moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the full extent of the damage.

3

Structural Drying

Commercial dehumidifiers and air movers dry every affected structural component, including roof decking, insulation, wall cavities and flooring, to prevent mold growth.

4

Rebuild and Permanent Repairs

We repair or replace the flooring, insulation, drywall, siding, roofing and anything else the storm damaged. When we finish, the building is returned to how it looked before the weather hit.

How to Tell You Need Storm Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wind-driven rain coming in through windows, doors or wall penetrations
  • Ceilings or walls that stain while it is storming or soon afterward
  • Ceiling drywall that sags or bulges, or paint that bubbles after rain
  • Attic or wall insulation that is damp or matted down following a storm
  • Flashing that is bent, loose or missing at roof edges, valleys and vents
  • Missing, damaged or lifted shingles you can see from the ground
  • Standing water in attic spaces, especially by skylights and chimneys

Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Learn where the main water shutoff is and how to close it if pipes are damaged in a storm
  • Clean out downspouts and gutters regularly, which lets heavy rainfall drain away as it should
  • Homes in hurricane country benefit from shutters or impact-rated window glass
  • Make sure the ground around the house slopes away and downspouts discharge well clear of it
  • Check window and door seals each year and replace any caulking that has cracked
  • Good attic insulation and ventilation stop ice dams from forming along the roof edge

Why Professional Storm Damage Repair Matters

Storm damage creates complex, connected repair needs that take experienced restoration professionals to handle properly. Wet insulation, soaked roof decking and damp wall cavities often sit behind a leak that appears small, and without proper detection and drying they bring mold and a weakening structure. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace water paths and identify every affected area.

Temporary protection such as tarps and board-up needs correct technique or it will not hold. Tarps put on the wrong way may funnel water into the building or tear loose in the wind. We have done a lot of storm mitigation, and we use suitable materials and methods so the temporary protection holds until permanent repairs are on the calendar.
