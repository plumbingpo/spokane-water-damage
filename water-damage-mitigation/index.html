Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Repair is a different job. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. In Spokane, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. They are two phases. Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the loss, limit the spread, stabilize the home. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. Source control, so no new water enters. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

Our Water Damage Mitigation Process

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Stop the Water Source

The supply gets shut off, the broken line capped, or the opening letting water in tarped and boarded. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Set Up Containment

Plastic barriers and controlled airflow separate wet rooms from dry ones. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

We remove what cannot be saved, treat surfaces with antimicrobial, and place dehumidifiers and air movers. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.

The Benefits of Professional Water Damage Mitigation

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • In cold months, a heated house protects pipes from freezing, even while you travel.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation of the house.
  • Check under sinks and around toilets monthly for drips, stains or soft flooring.
  • Have an automatic shutoff fitted, so the main closes itself once a leak is sensed.

When to Call for Water Damage Mitigation in Spokane

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rain is getting inside through a damaged roof or an open window gap.
  • One day on, damp materials are beginning to smell musty.
  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.
  • Windows keep fogging and the air feels humid days after the water loss.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • Bathroom or kitchen cabinets are sitting over water that cannot be reached to dry.
